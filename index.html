          Educational tool · Levine PhenoAge

          Biological age calculator

          Estimate your “phenotypic age” from nine routine blood markers and your age, using the published PhenoAge model (Levine et al., 2018). Use results from the same blood draw.

          Please read first

          This is a research and educational tool, not a diagnostic test. PhenoAge is a statistical estimate developed from population data, mainly from the United States. It does not diagnose, predict or rule out any disease, and it is not a substitute for medical advice. Results can be thrown off by a recent illness, infection, injury or hard exercise, and by some medications. Do not start, stop or change any treatment based on this result. Talk to your clinician about your blood work.

          Your privacy: the calculation runs entirely in your browser. Nothing you enter is saved, sent or stored, and it is cleared when you leave or refresh the page.

          How it's calculated

          PhenoAge combines your age with nine blood markers using the weights published by Levine and colleagues in 2018, which were derived from the US NHANES studies. The markers are converted to the units used in the original model (albumin g/L, creatinine µmol/L, glucose mmol/L, CRP mg/dL on a log scale, lymphocyte %, MCV fL, RDW %, ALP U/L and white cells × 10³/µL) and turned into an estimated mortality score, which is then expressed as an age.

          Source: Levine ME, et al. An epigenetic biomarker of aging for lifespan and healthspan. Aging (Albany NY). 2018;10(4):573–591.

          Want to look deeper?

          Your clinician can review your blood work with you, alongside other measures of how you're ageing, such as VO₂ max, Body Composition and arterial stiffness.

            Melasma, Sun Spots or PIH? How to Tell Dark Patches Apart

            By the Mediworks clinical team · 4 min read

            Hyperpigmentation means areas of skin that are darker than the skin around them. It happens when the skin makes extra melanin, the pigment that gives skin its colour. Three types are especially common, and each has different triggers.

            Sun spots

            Sun spots, also called solar lentigines, are flat, well-defined brown spots on the areas that get the most sun: the face, hands, chest and shoulders. They build up after years of UV exposure and become more common with age.

            Melasma

            Melasma appears as larger, often symmetrical patches on the cheeks, forehead, upper lip or chin. It is linked to hormones, such as pregnancy or the birth control pill, as well as sun, visible light and heat. It is more common in women and in medium to darker skin tones. Melasma tends to return, so it is usually managed over time.

            Post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation (PIH)

            PIH is the dark mark left after the skin has been inflamed or injured, for example by acne, eczema, a burn, a scratch or an overly aggressive treatment. It is more common, and often lasts longer, in darker skin tones. It may fade gradually once the inflammation is under control.

            Why the type matters

            Each type behaves differently, and treatments that irritate the skin can make melasma or PIH worse. That is why an assessment comes first. A spot that is new, changing, irregular or bleeding should always be checked by a doctor.

            How we approach pigment at Mediworks

            Daily broad-spectrum sunscreen is the foundation for every type. Options your clinician may consider include Medical-Grade Skincare and Microneedling paired with tranexamic acid, glutathione, PRP or Exosomes. For melasma, a Naturopathic Consultation can also look at hormone-related triggers. Results vary between people, and pigmentation can return.

            Neck Bands and the Nefertiti Lift: What to Know

            By the Mediworks clinical team · 3 min read

            The platysma is a thin sheet of muscle that runs from the collarbone up to the jaw. With age and repeated tension, it can form vertical bands that show when you talk, strain or exercise, and it can pull down on the jawline.

            What is a Nefertiti lift?

            Named after the Egyptian queen known for her defined jawline, the Nefertiti lift is a technique that places small doses of a Neuromodulator, a wrinkle-relaxing injection, along the jawline and into the platysmal bands. Relaxing the muscle reduces its downward pull on the lower face.

            Who considers it?

            It is usually discussed with people who have visible neck bands or early softening along the jaw. It does not remove loose skin or fat. If crepey skin or laxity is the main concern, other options may suit you better.

            Other options for the neck and décolletage

            For crepey skin and fine lines, your clinician may consider Sculptra, a collagen-stimulating injectable, or Microneedling, with Exosomes as an add-on.

            What to expect

            Treatment takes about 15 to 30 minutes. The effect usually becomes visible over about two weeks and commonly lasts three to four months. Bruising and tenderness can occur. Rarely, neck treatment can cause temporary neck weakness or difficulty swallowing, which your clinician reviews with you first. Injectables at Mediworks are performed only by our Naturopathic Doctors, after a Naturopathic Aesthetic Consultation.

            What Is Adaptive Contrast Therapy? A Look at LiveO2

            By the Mediworks clinical team · 3 min read

            LiveO2 Adaptive Contrast is an exercise-with-oxygen system. You ride a stationary bike while switching between oxygen-rich and reduced-oxygen air. Sessions take about 15 to 25 minutes, and your oxygen levels and heart rate are monitored throughout.

            Why switch between air types?

            Breathing oxygen-rich air builds a reserve of oxygen in the blood. Reduced-oxygen air prompts blood vessels to widen, and exercise raises heart rate and blood flow. The approach is designed to increase circulation and oxygen delivery to tissues.

            Who may it be included for?

            It may be part of a plan for brain fog and focus, supportive care after a concussion alongside your physician, energy and endurance, exercise recovery or healthy ageing goals.

            What does the research show?

            Research on LiveO2 itself is limited. Related approaches, such as intermittent hypoxia training and exercise with oxygen, have been studied, but many studies are small. Your clinician will explain what is currently known, so you can decide with clear expectations.

            Do I need to be fit?

            No. The intensity is adjusted to you. Your health is screened first, as some heart and lung conditions mean it isn't suitable.

            Before You Start Hormone Therapy: Six Things to Know

            By Dr. Navi Badesha, ND · 5 min readReviewed November 2026

            Hormone therapy can help when hormones are the real cause of symptoms. Getting there starts with a careful look at the whole picture. Here is what to expect before any treatment is discussed.

            1. Symptoms overlap

            Fatigue, low mood, poor sleep, low libido and weight change have many possible causes, including thyroid problems, low iron, sleep apnea, stress and some medications. A good assessment checks for these as well as hormones.

            2. Testing confirms the picture

            For men, low Testosterone is diagnosed from symptoms together with low morning Testosterone on at least two separate blood tests. For women in Perimenopause, the diagnosis rests mainly on age and symptoms, because hormone levels swing from day to day. Testing still helps rule out other causes and guides monitoring.

            3. Your health history matters

            A history of breast or prostate cancer, blood clots, or a recent heart attack or stroke changes what may be appropriate. Bring a full list of your medications and supplements to your first visit.

            4. Think about fertility

            Testosterone Therapy can reduce sperm production. If you are planning children, tell your clinician, since other approaches may be considered.

            5. Monitoring is part of treatment

            Expect follow-up visits and repeat bloodwork, more often in the first year, so your plan can be adjusted to your symptoms and results.

            6. Know the costs

            Mediworks is private pay. Naturopathic visits may be covered by extended health plans, and we direct bill where your plan allows. For TRT, most bloodwork is covered by MSP; Bioavailable Testosterone, Vitamin D and PSA are paid directly to LifeLabs. The TRT protocol fee is not eligible for extended health reimbursement, although your prescription may be. Your clinician explains costs before anything is ordered.

            LifeLabs Blood Tests or DUTCH? Choosing a Hormone Test

            By Dr. Navi Badesha, ND · 4 min readReviewed November 2026

            Hormones can be measured in blood or in urine, and each shows something different. Here is how the two main options at Mediworks compare.

            LifeLabs blood tests

            A blood draw gives a snapshot of hormone levels at that moment. It is the standard way to measure Testosterone, estradiol, FSH, LH and thyroid hormones, and it is used to monitor Testosterone Therapy. Timing matters: Testosterone is tested in the morning, and some female hormones on a set day of the cycle.

            The DUTCH test

            DUTCH, from Precision Analytical, uses dried urine samples collected at home over a day. As well as sex hormones, it shows how your body breaks them down (their metabolites) and the daily rhythm of cortisol, your main stress hormone. DUTCH Cycle Mapping tracks estrogen and progesterone across a whole month.

            What each can't do

            DUTCH does not test thyroid function, and it is not used to diagnose low Testosterone; blood tests are used for both. Urine and blood results are measured differently, so the numbers are not interchangeable.

            Which comes first?

            Most people start with bloodwork. DUTCH is considered when questions remain about cortisol patterns, how hormones are being processed, or changes across the cycle.

            Cost

            Both are paid privately, and some extended health plans reimburse lab fees. Your clinician recommends a test only if it will change your plan, and explains costs before ordering.

            Sources

            • Precision Analytical. DUTCH test information for patients (dutchtest.com).
            • LifeLabs. Test information (lifelabs.com).

            NAD+ IV Therapy: What It Is and What Research Shows

            By the Mediworks clinical team · 3 min read

            NAD+ (nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide) is a coenzyme found in every cell. Cells use it to produce energy and to support processes such as DNA repair. Levels decline with age.

            Why an IV, and why so slow?

            NAD+ IV Therapy delivers NAD+ directly into the bloodstream. Infusions usually take two to four hours, because a faster rate can cause chest tightness, nausea or flushing. If you feel these, the drip is slowed.

            Who considers it?

            It may be discussed for fatigue, brain fog, supportive care after a concussion and healthy ageing goals, usually as part of a wider plan.

            What does the research show?

            Human research on NAD+ infusions is still early, and much of what is known comes from laboratory and animal studies. Oral precursors, such as NR and NMN, are also being studied. Your clinician will be clear about what is and isn't known.

            Before your first infusion

            A health screen comes first, to confirm the infusion suits you. You can read, work on a laptop or rest during the session.

            Do I need testing before my visit?

            No, except for TRT, which starts with bloodwork. Otherwise, come as you are. Your clinician will decide at your first visit whether any testing would help, and which tests.             An Initial Naturopathic Consultation takes 45 to 60 minutes. If treatment is done the same day, allow extra time. We'll tell you how long to plan for when you book.

            Can I start with a virtual visit?

            Yes. Many Naturopathic consultations and follow-ups can be done virtually, and all TRT consultations are virtual. In-person visits are needed for injections, IVs, scans and some testing.

            Who will I see?

            Most care is led by our Naturopathic Doctors. TRT is managed by our MD and Nurse Practitioner, and our holistic nutritionist supports nutrition plans. You'll see the same team at follow-ups wherever possible.

            Baseline FAQ

            Questions about the Baseline.

            Is the Baseline right for me?

            It suits people who want to understand their current fitness, Body Composition and key biomarkers before making changes, whether the goal is energy, weight, performance or healthy ageing. Your clinician will confirm at your consultation.

            Do I need every test?

            No. Your clinician suggests only the assessments that are useful for your history and goals. Some people start with blood work and one in-clinic test.

            What is biological age?

            An estimate of how your body is ageing, based on routine blood markers rather than your birthday. It is an educational estimate, not a diagnosis, and is most useful for tracking change over time.

            Do I need to fast?

            Some blood tests, such as fasting glucose, insulin and lipids, need a fast. We'll tell you exactly how to prepare for each test when you book.

            How often should I re-test?

            Most patients re-test every 6–12 months, depending on their goals and plan.

            Is it covered by insurance?

            Naturopathic visits may be covered by extended health plans, and we offer direct billing. Lab tests and scans are usually not covered, so your clinician will go over costs with you before anything is ordered.
